IRRIGATION GUIDE FOR FARMERS

Irrigation means supplying water to crops when natural rainfall is not enough.

1. Importance of Irrigation

Proper irrigation helps crops:
- Maintain healthy growth.
- Absorb nutrients from the soil.
- Reduce water stress.
- Improve crop productivity when managed properly.

2. Common Irrigation Methods

Drip Irrigation:
- Water is delivered slowly near the plant roots.
- It can reduce water loss through evaporation and runoff.
- It is useful for many vegetable, fruit, and row crops.

Sprinkler Irrigation:
- Water is distributed through sprinklers.
- It can be useful for many field and horticultural crops.
- Proper scheduling is important to avoid unnecessary water use.

Flood or Surface Irrigation:
- Water is distributed over the soil surface.
- It is commonly used for some field crops.
- Poor management can result in water loss and waterlogging.

3. Irrigation Scheduling

The amount and frequency of irrigation depend on:
- Crop type.
- Soil type.
- Crop growth stage.
- Weather conditions.
- Rainfall.
- Temperature.
- Available water.

Farmers should avoid both under-irrigation and excessive irrigation.

4. Water Conservation

Farmers can improve water efficiency by:
- Using drip irrigation where suitable.
- Irrigating according to crop requirements.
- Checking soil moisture before irrigation.
- Maintaining irrigation equipment properly.
- Reducing unnecessary runoff and evaporation.

IMPORTANT:
There is no single irrigation schedule suitable for every farm. Farmers should consider local weather, soil conditions, crop requirements, and recommendations from agricultural experts.
